### 1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?

The selected plant is Citrus sinensis (L.) Osbeck, commonly known as sweet orange, which belongs to the family Rutaceae. Its complete chloroplast genome was obtained from the NCBI RefSeq database under accession number NC_008334.1.

### 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?

The complete chloroplast genome of Citrus sinensis is 160,129 base pairs (bp) long. This genome length was confirmed using the NCBI GenBank record and Galaxy gfastats analysis from the previous laboratory activity.

### 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.

Yes. The circular plastid genome map shows the typical quadripartite organization consisting of the Large Single-Copy (LSC), Small Single-Copy (SSC), and two Inverted Repeat regions (IRa and IRb). The LSC occupies the largest section toward the upper portion of the circular map, while the SSC is located at the bottom. The two inverted repeats flank the SSC, with IRa on the lower-right side and IRb on the lower-left side of the map.

### 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.

Three examples of genes located in the LSC region are *psbA, psaA, and rbcL*. The psbA gene encodes the D1 protein of Photosystem II, psaA encodes a core protein of Photosystem I, and rbcL encodes the large subunit of RuBisCO involved in carbon fixation. These genes contribute to important processes associated with photosynthesis.

### 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.

One example of a gene located in the SSC region is *ndhA*, which encodes a subunit of the chloroplast NADH dehydrogenase-like complex. This protein complex is involved in photosynthetic electron transport. Other examples of genes in the SSC region include ndhD and psaC.

### 6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?

The *rpl2* gene is located within the inverted-repeat regions of the Citrus sinensis chloroplast genome. It encodes a protein component of the large ribosomal subunit, which is involved in protein synthesis. The gene occurs in both IRa and IRb, resulting in two copies because the inverted repeats contain duplicated genomic sequences.

### 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.

The *psbA* gene is a photosynthesis-related gene visible on the plastid genome map. It encodes the D1 reaction-center protein of Photosystem II, which plays an essential role in the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis. The D1 protein participates in the transfer of electrons during the conversion of light energy into chemical energy.

### 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?

The direction of the gene arrows indicates the orientation of genes and the direction in which they are transcribed. Genes located on opposite DNA strands can be transcribed in opposite directions around the circular plastid genome. This illustrates how genes are organized on both strands of chloroplast DNA.

### 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.

The GC content graph shows slight variations around the circular chloroplast genome rather than a completely uniform pattern. Some regions display higher or lower GC content, indicating differences in nucleotide composition across the genome. The overall GC content is 38.48%, as determined in the previous Galaxy analysis. These variations reflect differences in sequence composition, although the map alone cannot establish their biological significance.

### 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?

A graphical plastid genome map provides a clearer representation of genome organization by showing the positions, functions, and orientations of annotated genes. It also makes it easier to identify major structural regions, duplicated genes, and variations in GC content. Compared with raw DNA sequences or lengthy annotation lists, the map allows researchers to understand the overall genome structure more efficiently.

## Summary

The circular plastid genome map of Citrus sinensis illustrates a complete chloroplast genome measuring 160,129 bp. It displays the typical quadripartite organization consisting of the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions. Important genes involved in photosynthesis, transcription, translation, and other plastid functions are distributed throughout the genome.

The map also illustrates gene orientation, duplicated genes within the inverted-repeat regions, and variations in GC content. Overall, OGDRAW provided a clear visualization of the structural and functional organization of the Citrus sinensis chloroplast genome.
